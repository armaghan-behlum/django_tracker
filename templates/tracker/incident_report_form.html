Discrimination & Censorship Tracker

Need help filling out this form? Call us at 415-861-7444 between 10AM and 5PM PT. A member of our staff can help you fill it out or arrange time to fill it out together.

This is a national tracker for incidents of hate, racism, or repression against Palestinian, Arab, allied, and other BAMEMSA individuals. It covers schools and other settings across the country. Reporting here helps us fight back against the growing racism and repression facing our communities and our kids.

AROC Action and researchers from the Institute for Understanding Anti-Palestinian Racism (IUAPR) developed this tool together. We will use it to understand and challenge the many types of discrimination our communities face.

For individuals in California, this tracker also allows you to choose if you want to submit to statewide incident registries that help us respond to unfair laws and racist trends in California schools and other settings. Californians can also choose if they want to receive direct follow-up support from AROC or legal advisors at K-12 Defense, PalLegal and/or CAIR. This form does not in itself constitute legal advice.

Privacy and Anonymity

Nothing you submit will be identified with you and shared anywhere beyond the research team without your permission. When you fill out this form, all of the information is encrypted and kept secure, private, and confidential. Any incident data will be anonymized so that nothing can be traced back to you unless you want to share your story publicly.

Read more about how the data is securely collected and stored

About This Registry: This registry is coordinated by the AROC Action, K-12 Legal Defense, and the IUAPR Research Team. It is a data collection effort focused on experiences of censorship, discrimination and racism, especially anti-Palestinian racism, in K-12 school and other settings. Participation is entirely voluntary. If you decide you no longer want to share your experience, you may exit the survey before submitting and your data will be deleted. You may also skip several questions. If you have questions before deciding whether to participate, please contact us at research@iuapr.org before proceeding.

Who Can Participate: This registry is open to anyone aged 13 or older who has experienced or witnessed anti-Palestinian racism or other forms of discrimination or hatred in a K-12 school or other settings, including students, parents, guardians, teachers, and school staff.

What Information We Collect: To participate, you will be asked to provide your name, email address, and zip code. This information is used to verify respondents and may be used to contact you for follow-up purposes. You may also opt out of any contact. Your identifying information will be kept confidential, will not be shared publicly, and will not be shared with outside parties. Only the coordinators of this project will have access to your identifying information.

Follow-up: By agreeing to fill out this form, you are consenting to potentially having one of our team members contact you for further information, if needed.

If you are seeking support or legal assistance in California, please use this survey to opt-in to seek direct follow-up support from AROC or referral for legal assistance from K-12 Legal Defense, CAIR, or Palestine Legal. If you are seeking support in other states, please opt-in to share your information with Palestine Legal and AROC Action.

Benefits: By sharing your experience, you will be contributing to a growing body of knowledge that helps establish the scale of discrimination, censorship, and harm we have seen growing against Palestinian, Arab, allied, and other BAMEMSA individuals. It also helps us specifically establish anti-Palestinian racism as a recognized and documented harm. This benefits the broader Palestinian and allied community by supporting advocacy for policy change: for example, in K-12 schools, informing educational resources, and strengthening efforts to address anti-Palestinian racism across all sectors of civil society.

Risks: While this form is encrypted and your personal information is de-identified when viewed by our researchers, sharing experiences of discrimination and anti-Palestinian racism may carry personal risks, including potential social, professional, or institutional repercussions if your identity were ever disclosed. The IUAPR Research Team, AROC Action and K-12 Legal Defense is committed to protecting your confidentiality and will take all reasonable steps to safeguard your information, including by deleting all personally-identifying information after a year. However, no data collection system can guarantee absolute security, and you should be aware of this before participating.

How Your Data Will Be Used: Registry data will be used to compile reports about the nature and extent of anti-Palestinian racism in K-12 schools, to develop educational resources, to advocate for policy change, support legislative testimony, media or research reports, or for other purposes. Your information will not be used to identify you individually unless you provide express permission for this. Descriptions of experiences you share may be quoted directly in publications or policy materials. We will take all reasonable steps to eliminate the risk of quoted material being traced back to you, unless you have explicitly given permission to be identified. Your de-identified data will be retained indefinitely and may be used for future publications and policy advocacy related to anti-Palestinian racism in K-12 schools.

Data Security: Your identifying information will be encrypted and stored securely, with access limited to coordinators between the IUAPR Research Team and AROC Action. All non-identifying data submitted to this form is encrypted and kept secure and confidential to the research teams. Nothing you submit about your experiences will be identified with you or shared externally without your permission.

FOR PARTICIPANTS AGED 13–17 — Please Read This Section

We want to make sure you understand what you are agreeing to before you take this survey. Here is what you need to know:

This survey is about anti-Palestinian racism and other forms of discrimination and hatred. This includes things you may have seen, heard, or experienced at school because of your Palestinian identity or someone you know who is Palestinian, or because of your support for Palestinian rights.

If we ever quote what you wrote, we will ensure it cannot be traced back to you. We will make every effort to keep your name and information private and will not share it with your school, your teachers, or anyone else outside our team without your consent.

If you decide you no longer want to share your experience, you may exit the survey before submitting and your data will be deleted. You may also skip several questions. We require certain questions to ensure accurate and consistent details are collected, but all personal information is removed when researchers review the responses. Without identifying you in any way, your answers may be used in reports or publications to help show that anti-Arab, anti-Muslim, or anti-Palestinian racism in schools is a real and serious problem.

By checking the "consent" box you are providing your informed assent to participate in this registry.

What is anti-Palestinian racism?

Anti-Palestinian racism is a form of systemic racism that works to silence, exclude, erase, stereotype, defame, or treat Palestinians and their stories as less than human. It shows up in many ways and can include:

Nakba Denial
Discrediting the factual basis of Palestinian expulsion in 1948 and other massacres.
Justifying Violence Against Palestinians
E.g. bullying or threatening a student for wearing a keffiyeh or standing up for Palestinian rights.
Denying Palestinians are Indigenous to Palestine
With their own identity and rights.
Erasing Palestinian Human Rights
And their equal dignity and worth, e.g. not being allowed to talk about what is happening to Palestinians, related news stories or experiences.
Excluding Palestinians or Palestinian perspectives
And stories, or pressuring others to exclude them, e.g. not being allowed to join a club or host an event.
Defaming Palestinians or their allies
With slander, such as calling them inherently anti-semitic, a terrorist threat or supporter, against democracy, or falsely associating them with Palestinian political factions.
